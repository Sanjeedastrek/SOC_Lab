The tools matter less than understanding permissions, networking, and reading logs methodically. However, Splunk is generally more stable due to maturity and established practices. On the other hand, Sentinel offers better Azure integration with Microsoft ecosystem at the cost of frequent changes and updates. 

This project exposed that reliable log collection and forwarding can break down in implementation. Typos in inputs.conf, port 9997 blocked by firewall, the forwarder lacking permissions to read Sysmon logs, timezone mismatches between my system and Sentinel, and line breaks in PowerShell scripts, each stopped progress for hours. I learned to troubleshoot systematically: check one component at a time, find error logs, and read them carefully. I also practiced full report writing using Obsidian, touched SPL and KQL a bit (though KQL seemed more straightforward), and mapped attacker behaviour to MITRE ATT&CK techniques. This lab showed me what SOC analysts actually encounter daily.
